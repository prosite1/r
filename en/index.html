Dr Rached Hamza

Specialist physician in Angiology

Dr Rached Hamza is a vascular physician and phlebologist in Sfax. His practice covers varicose veins, veins and arteries: diagnosis by duplex ultrasound, then treatment — most often without surgery.

Diagnosis and treatment happen in the same place: duplex ultrasound and capillaroscopy for the assessment, then foam sclerotherapy, endovenous laser, radiofrequency or phlebectomy depending on the vein involved. Procedures are day cases under local anaesthetic, with a quick return to normal activity. The practice is CNAM-affiliated.

  • Foam sclerotherapy and microsclerotherapy

Services

From diagnosis to treatment — the whole vascular pathway under one roof. Click a service to see what it involves.

Vascular consultation

History taking, full clinical examination and vascular risk assessment, with a care plan explained and given to you in writing.

Vascular duplex ultrasound

Arterial and venous assessment of the limbs, aorta and carotid arteries. Painless, no injection, immediate result.

Sclerotherapy for varicose veins

Injection treatment (liquid or ultrasound-guided foam) of varicose veins and thread veins. Short sessions, no time off work.

Endovenous laser & radiofrequency

Thermal closure of diseased saphenous veins — an alternative to surgery: local anaesthetic, day case, walking straight away.

Chronic venous insufficiency

Heavy legs, swelling, night cramps: haemodynamic assessment, properly fitted medical compression and regular follow-up.

Venous thromboembolism

Urgent diagnosis of deep vein thrombosis, starting and monitoring anticoagulation, and prevention of recurrence.

Peripheral artery disease

Pain on walking, ankle-brachial pressure index, screening for atherosclerosis and referral for revascularisation when needed.

Compression therapy

Pneumatic compression therapy and made-to-measure medical compression for heavy legs, swelling and maintaining results.

Ulcers & chronic wounds

Identifying the cause of the wound, a clear dressing protocol, compression, and treatment of the underlying venous or arterial disease.

Aesthetic phlebology & cutaneous laser

Clearing thread veins and small visible vessels on the legs and face, using cutaneous laser or microsclerotherapy.

You may be wondering

Frequently asked questions

Answers to the questions most often asked in a vascular consultation.

What does a vascular physician do?

They are the specialist for blood vessels: arteries, veins and lymphatics. They diagnose and treat varicose veins, thrombosis, artery disease, lymphoedema and ulcers, and perform the duplex ultrasound themselves.

What are varicose veins?

They are dilated, twisted veins visible under the skin, most often on the legs. The valves that stop blood from flowing back down no longer work properly: blood pools and the vein stretches. They can cause heavy legs, swelling or cramps, and tend to worsen over time without treatment.

Is a duplex ultrasound painful? Do I need to fast?

The scan is completely painless and radiation-free: just a probe and warm gel on the skin. No injection and no fasting for leg scans. Allow 20–30 minutes and wear clothes that are easy to remove.

What treatments are available for varicose veins today?

It depends on the vein: ultrasound-guided foam sclerotherapy, endovenous laser, radiofrequency, vein glue or — more rarely — surgery. Most are done in the clinic under local anaesthetic, and you walk out afterwards.

Heavy legs: when should I see a doctor?

As soon as the discomfort is daily: evening swelling, night cramps, itching, visible veins that keep enlarging, or a change in skin colour around the ankle. The earlier the assessment, the simpler the treatment.

Does sclerotherapy hurt? How many sessions?

The injection feels much like a blood test; no anaesthetic is needed. On average 2 to 4 sessions a few weeks apart, depending on how extensive the veins are. Compression is worn for a few days after each session.

Do I have to wear compression stockings, and for how long?

Compression is the foundation of venous treatment: it relieves symptoms and prevents complications. The class and duration are decided after the ultrasound — a few days after a procedure, or daily in advanced venous insufficiency.

What are the warning signs of deep vein thrombosis?

A painful, firm, warm and swollen calf on one side only, especially after immobility, a long journey or surgery. Sudden breathlessness or chest pain is an emergency: call the emergency services immediately.

Can I exercise with varicose veins?

Yes — it is recommended: walking, swimming and cycling activate the calf muscle pump. Limit prolonged standing, excessive heat, heavy lifting and repeated high-impact sport if the veins are large.

How should I prepare for my consultation?

Bring your current prescriptions, previous investigations (ultrasound, blood tests) and the list of any anticoagulants you take, and come with no cream on your legs. Please arrive 10 minutes before your appointment.
